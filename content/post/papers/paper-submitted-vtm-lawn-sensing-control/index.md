We submitted a new paper to the **IEEE Vehicular Technology Magazine, special issue on low-altitude wireless networks**:

### From Detection to Collision Avoidance: Closing the Sensing-Control Loop in Low-Altitude Wireless Networks
*Evangelos Vlachos*

Low-altitude wireless networks must support logistics, air taxis, and aerial sensing over cities while sharing space with road traffic and cellular infrastructure. Network sensing often stops at detection but safe operation requires a separation margin that an avoidance function can maintain given surveillance accuracy and latency. This tutorial frames sensing, communication, and control as a single loop so that the network can meet U-space requirements and be treated as an aviation surveillance source. We review operational requirements and 3GPP sensing provisions and identify where those provisions leave gaps. We show how sensing error and delivery delay combine into a separation margin that a control barrier function can enforce and present a worked example that links observation time and cooperating sites to the achieved safety margin. We conclude with where learning fits and what trials and standards steps are needed to move from demonstrations to accepted surveillance services.

Key contributions:
- **Loop perspective**: present sensing, communication, and control as a unified design problem.
- **Analysis of requirements**: compare U-space and 3GPP provisions and expose gaps for safety.
- **Design guidance**: show how sensing error and delay map to a separation margin and practical trade offs.
